Ticket 05 · vertical slice

Due dates: the ledger, the overdue stamp, mark done

ready-for-agent

Opening the app answers “what needs doing” with no navigation: every pet’s outstanding due dates on one screen, oldest problem first, anything past its date stamped with how many days it is overdue.

Blocked by

04  An entry exists: the universal log form and the feed

What to build

This ledger is the entire reminder system. Paw Pages sends no email and no notification, which is why it never needs a phone number and why opening the app is the only thing that has to happen — and in turn why the ledger sits at the top of both the home screen and every pet’s page, and why the overdue stamp is loud.

Due and overdue are computed once, in the database. Every screen reads the same view; the backend never reimplements “overdue” and neither does the frontend — the overdue flag and the day count arrive from Postgres. The archived-pet exclusion lives inside the view, so no endpoint has to remember it and the app can never show an overdue booster in red for a dog that has died.

The home screen is the app’s most-loaded page and is one request: the ledger rows sorted by due date ascending, the pet cards with each pet’s next due and last logged date, the three summary counts, and the archived count — not three round trips.

Two actions close an outstanding item. Mark done stops it appearing without logging anything, for something dealt with elsewhere; the entry stays in the history. “Log the next one” handles the common case — the booster was given — by pre-filling the form from the outstanding item, then creating the new entry and closing the old due date in the same request. Nothing closes automatically: free-text titles mean the app cannot know that two entries are the same series.

Endpoints in this slice

GET/dashboard
POST/entries/{id}/mark-done

Acceptance criteria

  • The home screen loads its ledger, pet cards, summary counts and archived count in a single request
  • The ledger orders oldest problem first, across all of the handler’s pets
  • Overdue is exactly due_on < today and due_closed_at is null, tested at the boundary days — the day before, the day of, and the day after
  • Anything overdue carries the stamp and the number of days it is overdue
  • The summary line shows active pets, overdue items, and items due within thirty days
  • Each pet’s page opens with what that pet needs, reading the same view as the home ledger
  • An entry whose due date has passed is marked overdue in the feed too, so the feed and the ledger cannot tell the handler different things
  • Mark done drops the item from the ledger and leaves the entry in the history
  • “Log the next one” creates an entry and closes the old due date together, in one request
  • Nothing in the app sends an email, a text or a notification
  • Stamp red appears only on something overdue, at most once per screen
  • Every date renders in the monospace face
  • The ledger and the needs-attention panel each have a desktop and a mobile layout

Done means

Every criterion above passes at the two seams the PRD fixed: the HTTP API against a real Postgres with RLS, the views and the constraints all live, and the rendered screen with HTTP stubbed at the network boundary. No test mocks the database, no test mocks a hook, and no test asserts on component internals — a test that breaks when nothing observable changed is a bug in the test. The desktop and mobile layouts are built together in this slice, not as a pass at the end: the slice is not done until both exist.
