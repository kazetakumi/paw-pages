Ticket 04 · vertical slice

An entry exists: the universal log form and the feed

ready-for-agent

One form for a rabies booster, a vet visit and a nail trim — a date, what happened in the handler’s own words, and optionally the vet, a note, and when the next one is due.

Blocked by

03  A pet exists: add, list, About tab

What to build

There is nothing to learn and nothing to choose before starting. The title is typed, never picked from a list, so the app can never tell a handler their pet’s event is not a valid category. The titles they have used recently come back as one-tap chips — the distinct titles from their own last entries across all their pets, capped at four — so repeat events are two taps rather than retyping. There is no global vocabulary and no suggestion the handler did not write themselves. Today and Yesterday shortcuts mean logging something as it happens takes no date entry at all, and a future date is refused: the record only ever contains things that have actually happened.

The vet or clinic is recorded on the entry itself, not on the pet, so changing vets does not rewrite the history of who did what. The note is free text — a batch number, what the vet said, how she took the tablet — captured while the handler still remembers it.

A next-due date is optional, and the form says in plain words what setting one will do: it appears on the home screen, it stays there until the next one is logged, and it never sends an email. Interval shortcuts of +1, +3 and +6 months and +1 and +3 years set it from the vet’s stated interval without calendar arithmetic. “Nothing due after this” says so explicitly, so a one-off grooming is not an unfinished form. A due date before the date the thing happened cannot be saved.

The pet’s page becomes the feed by default — the record first, not a settings screen. Newest entry at the top, each showing its date, title, note, vet and next due. Older entries collapse behind a “show older” control so a pet with years of history still opens on what is recent; the history paginates by an opaque cursor over the existing feed index rather than by offset.

Endpoints in this slice

POST/entries
PATCH/entries/{id}
DELETE/entries/{id}
GET/pets/{id}/entries
GET/entry-titles/recent

Acceptance criteria

  • An entry saves with a date and a description alone
  • The same form creates and edits, behind one Save button
  • Opening the form from a pet’s page pre-selects that pet
  • Today and Yesterday set the date with no date entry
  • A happened_on in the future is a 422, not a 500
  • A due_on before happened_on is a 422
  • The five interval shortcuts each produce the correct next-due date from the entry date
  • “Nothing due after this” clears the due date and the explanatory panel
  • The form states that a due date appears on the home screen, stays until the next one is logged, and never triggers an email
  • Recent-title chips populate the title field, come from the handler’s own last entries across all their pets, and are capped at four
  • Vet and note save on the entry and appear in the feed
  • An entry can be edited and deleted after saving
  • The feed shows newest first with date, title, note, vet and next due
  • The feed collapses older entries and expands them on demand, paginating by cursor over (happened_on desc, id desc)
  • A second handler cannot read, update or delete any of the first handler’s entries
  • Field-level 422s surface next to the right field
  • The log form and the feed each have a desktop and a mobile layout

Done means

Every criterion above passes at the two seams the PRD fixed: the HTTP API against a real Postgres with RLS, the views and the constraints all live, and the rendered screen with HTTP stubbed at the network boundary. No test mocks the database, no test mocks a hook, and no test asserts on component internals — a test that breaks when nothing observable changed is a bug in the test. The desktop and mobile layouts are built together in this slice, not as a pass at the end: the slice is not done until both exist.
