Ticket 08 · vertical slice

Archiving and restore

done

A pet that passes away or is rehomed is archived with a reason. It keeps its history and its photo, stops counting toward due dates, and its public page goes dark.

Blocked by

05  Due dates: the ledger, the overdue stamp, mark done06  The public page

What to build

No handler should be asked to delete a pet in order to stop tracking it. Archiving takes a reason — passed away, rehomed, or other — so the record reflects what actually happened, and the confirm panel states plainly what will happen before it happens, because this is the worst possible moment to be surprised by any of it.

Archived pets are listed with their reason and date so they remain findable, and can be restored — a rehoming that fell through, or a mistaken tap, costs nothing. On the home screen they appear as a quiet count rather than as cards: present without being in the way.

The ledger exclusion already lives inside the due-dates view, so archiving a pet removes its items from every count without any endpoint remembering to filter. The same is true of the public views and, through the storage policy, of the photo route.

Endpoints in this slice

POST/pets/{id}/archive
POST/pets/{id}/restore

Acceptance criteria

  • Archiving takes one of the three reasons; an archive with no reason is rejected
  • The confirm panel states its consequences before confirming — history and photo kept, excluded from due dates, public page offline, reversible
  • Archiving removes the pet from the ledger and 404s its public page in the same request
  • Restoring puts both back
  • An archived pet keeps every entry and its photo
  • Archived pets are excluded from all three summary counts
  • Archived pets are listed with their reason and the date they were archived
  • The home screen shows a count of archived pets, not the pets themselves
  • The archive panel and the archived list each have a desktop and a mobile layout

Done means

Every criterion above passes at the two seams the PRD fixed: the HTTP API against a real Postgres with RLS, the views and the constraints all live, and the rendered screen with HTTP stubbed at the network boundary. No test mocks the database, no test mocks a hook, and no test asserts on component internals — a test that breaks when nothing observable changed is a bug in the test. The desktop and mobile layouts are built together in this slice, not as a pass at the end: the slice is not done until both exist.
