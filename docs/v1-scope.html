Paw Pages

A web app where a pet owner keeps a written record for each of their pets — profile, vaccinations, and everything else worth logging — and can hand out a public page for any one of them.

Status  Designed · scope not final Updated  30 August 2026 Screens  8, web and mobile Open  1 blocking · 5 questions

What it is

The shape of the thing, in one paragraph.

Anyone can sign up as a handler — which in practice means the pet's owner; the word is just vocabulary. A handler creates one or more pets, fills in a profile with a photo, and logs dated entries against each pet. Entries are not restricted to vaccinations: vet visits, medication, deworming, grooming and anything else all go in the same log. An entry can carry a next-due date, typed by hand. The app never emails or notifies — the dates are there when you open it, and overdue ones are stamped. Each pet can have a public read-only page at its own URL.

Product decisions

Settled in the interview. Reference codes point back to the question asked.

  1. It is a written log, not a recordingQ1 No video, no audio, no camera. "Record" means keeping a record.
  2. One handler, many petsQ17 A handler is the pet's own owner — not a walker, kennel or shelter managing other people's animals.
  3. Open signupQ2 Anyone can register. Real auth, real password reset, real isolation between handlers' data.
  4. Due dates are display-onlyQ3 No email, no push, no scheduled sending. Opening the app is the reminder.
  5. Responsive web appQ4 One codebase, works in a phone browser. No native app, no install, no offline.
  6. Entries cover everythingQ7 Vaccines, vet visits, medication, deworming, grooming, non-health events. Not a vaccine-only tracker.
  7. Entry type is free textQ13 The handler types whatever they want. No fixed list, no categories.
  8. Next due dates are typed by handQ11 The app knows no vaccine schedules and computes nothing. The handler copies the date off the vet's card.
  9. A public read-only page per petQ9 Each pet can have its own URL, shareable with anyone. Off by default.
  10. One profile photo per petQ15 No galleries, no attachments on entries, no scanned vaccination cards.
  11. Archive with a reasonQ18 Passed away, rehomed, or other. Archived pets keep their history but stop counting toward due dates.
  12. Account deletion and data exportQ19 A handler can erase everything, or download their pets and entries as one file.
  13. Clean slateQ5 The pre-existing database schema is abandoned, not adapted. Nothing carries over.

Settled by drawing the screens

These were parked during the interview and answered by the design. They are decisions, not sketches — but they were made in pixels rather than in conversation, so they are the ones most worth a second look.

  1. One universal entry formQ8 Pet, what happened, date, next due, vet, notes. Nothing changes per type — a rabies shot and a nail trim fill in the same fields.
  2. Home screen shows due dates above petsQ14 A cross-pet ledger of what is due or overdue, then the pet cards. With no notifications, this screen is the entire reminder system.
  3. A pet has an identity, and that is allQ16 Species, breed, sex, date of birth with an "approximate" flag, colour. Age is always derived, never stored.
  4. No microchip field Cut entirely. It was the only genuinely sensitive thing the app held, and removing it made the public page far easier to reason about.
  5. No vet on the pet Vet and clinic are recorded per entry instead. More accurate — pets change vets, and an old entry should keep whoever actually did it.
  6. Pet page splits into Feed and About The pet page is the feed: hero, what needs attention, then history. Identity, the public-page switch and archiving live behind an About tab.
  7. The public page shows the recordQ10 Photo, name, species, breed, colour, age, and every entry as a date and a title. Notes, vet, clinic and the owner's details are stripped.
  8. Archiving takes the public page offline Decided as a rider on archiving: the link goes dark rather than becoming a memorial. Archiving is undoable.
  9. Handler account holds optional personal details Name, email and password are required. Date of birth (age is derived), gender and nationality are optional and unset by default.
  10. Signup asks for three things Name, email, password. Everything else is filled in later, on the account page, or never.
  11. No bottom navigation on mobile The app is a shallow hierarchy, not a set of sections. One sticky primary action instead, and account behind the avatar.
  12. Forms commit with Save One label for both creating and editing, rather than an action-specific verb that changes between the two.

Design direction

Drawn from the vet's vaccination card — the stamped booklet with ruled date columns that this app replaces.

Colour. Passport ink on cool grey paper. Stamp red appears only on something overdue; vet green only on something current. Deliberately not the warm cream-and-terracotta look used for this document — that is a printed report, the app is a tool.

#13202E ink #F2F3EF paper #DCE0E4 rule #B23A24 stamp #2E6B4F current

Type. Archivo for display, Public Sans for body, and Space Mono for every date. The whole app is dates; setting them as data rather than prose is the one type decision doing real work.

Signature. A rubber-stamp impression reading OVERDUE, rotated a few degrees, with the day count beside it. It appears once per screen at most, only on the thing that is actually wrong. Since the app never notifies, overdue has to be impossible to miss on sight.

Not in v1

Proposed exclusions. Their status depends on the blocking question below — treat this list as provisional.

  • Email or push reminders
  • Vaccine schedule intelligence / computed due dates
  • Weight tracking and charts
  • Attachments on entries (card scans, receipts)
  • Photo galleries, multiple pet photos
  • Co-handlers and household sharing
  • Search or filtering across entries
  • Printable per-pet PDF for a vet or kennel
  • Native, mobile or offline builds
  • Cost tracking and spend per pet

Blocking question

Scope cannot be signed off while this is ambiguous.

Q20bDo the ten exclusions stay out, or come in? Blocking

The answer given — "keep those as well" — reads both ways, and the two readings are opposites. Keeping them out leaves the app exactly as designed. Pulling them in makes it roughly three times larger.

Pulling them in also contradicts decisions already made and drawn: reminders reverse decision 4 and undo the landing page's central claim, attachments reverse decision 10, and weight charts reopen decision 14. Reminders are the single largest jump — they reintroduce a background sending system with delivery, deduplication and opt-outs.

Still open

Smaller than the blocker, but each one changes something visible.

Q12Repeat dose — new entry or update? Assumed

Never formally answered, but the design assumes one: a new entry each time, with the old due date closed by hand through a Mark done control. Free-text entry types mean the app cannot recognise two entries as the same series, so nothing can close automatically.

Standing assumption New entry, history kept, old due date closed manually. Confirm or overturn.
Who signs the public page? Detail

It currently reads "Shared by Biscuit's owner" — no name at all. Attribution would make the record more credible to a kennel; a full name on a public URL is more identity than the page needs. First name is the middle ground.

Does overdue show as overdue to a visitor? Detail

The public page prints the due date in neutral grey with no stamp, on the grounds that publishing it turns the page into an accusation. A kennel would argue it is exactly what they need to know.

Is every entry public, or only some? Scope

The public page currently lists all entries, grooming and vet visits included. Limiting it to shots and treatments would need a per-entry visibility switch — real UI on the log form, and a v1 cost.

How are gender and nationality entered? Detail

A short list with "prefer not to say", or free text; a country picker, or free text. Neither field drives any behaviour in the app, so both are optional and unset by default.

Knock-on effects

Consequences already locked in by decisions made. Listed so they are not rediscovered later as bugs.

DecisionConsequence
Free-text entry types The app cannot tell that two entries belong to the same series, so due dates can never close automatically — hence the manual Mark done. It also rules out grouping, filtering, and any computed "Rabies — current" badge on the public page.
Display-only due dates Nothing reaches the handler unprompted. The value of the app depends entirely on them opening it, which is why the ledger sits at the top of both the home screen and each pet's page.
Archiving with a reason Archived pets are excluded from due-date calculations and their public page goes offline. An overdue booster shown in red for a pet that has died is a cruel failure, not a cosmetic one.
Open signup Strangers' data from day one. Isolation between handlers, password reset, export and account deletion stop being optional polish.
Optional personal details Gender and nationality drive nothing in the app, so they are pure liability: collected, stored, and deletable on request. The delete-account screen names them explicitly.
Public pet pages Anything on that URL is effectively published — forwardable, screenshot-able, potentially indexed. Removing the microchip field took the sharpest edge off this.

What happens next

The shortest path from here to a buildable scope.

One. Answer Q20b — out, in, or some of the ten. Nothing else can be sized until this is unambiguous.

Two. Confirm or overturn the twelve decisions the design made on your behalf, particularly the universal entry form and what the public page publishes.

Three. Settle the four smaller open questions — page attribution, public overdue, per-entry visibility, and how the optional fields are entered.

Four. Only then, technology. The stack question was raised and deliberately set aside; both sessions so far have been about concepts and screens.
